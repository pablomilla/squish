# The same question, with the salt case fixed

Ten `lookup` cases at ten repetitions, Opus 5 against Sonnet 5, 22 September
2026 — a repeat of `lookup-confirm/` after `salt` was marked against the target
the app actually hands the model rather than the 6 g national guideline, which
appears nowhere in it.

|  | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Opus 5, medium | 81% | **92%** |
| Sonnet 5, medium | 71% | **78%** |

One case was costing Opus ten points of its own score. `salt` went 0/10 to
10/10 and 2/10 to 10/10; the answers never changed.

| case | Opus | Sonnet |
|---|---|---|
| salt | 0 → 10 | 2 → 10 |
| weekends | 7 → 10 | **0 → 0** |
| fibre, usual-breakfast | 10 → 10 | 8 → 10 |
| days-logged, last-fish, named-day, worst-day | 10 → 10 | 10 → 10 |
| week-summary | 10 → 9 | 7 → 6 |
| **protein-trend** | **4 → 3** | **6 → 2** |

With `salt` no longer suppressing both, the gap is now significant: 92 against
78, p = 0.006. It is still one case. Drop `weekends` and it goes: 82/90 against
78/90, p = 0.34.

`weekends` is the finding. Sonnet has now scored 0/10 on it twice, twenty runs
without a pass. It reads the right days and reports two ranges without ever
subtracting one from the other, which is what the question asks for.

`protein-trend` is not a finding about models — it is `salt` again. The
question names no window, the claim pins "roughly 93 g a day at the start"
(the first seven days), and the model answers from mid-April at around 103 g.
The ending figure matches. Unlike `salt` this case carries no code check at
all, so there is no tolerance anywhere and the claim's exact figures are the
only gate. It should be fixed the same way before the lookup number is quoted
again.
